How to use it
  1. Add states by double-clicking the canvas or with Add state.
  2. Create a transition by dragging from the edge of a state to another state (or to itself, for a loop).
  3. Click the transition and write its condition with ¬ (NOT), ∧ (AND) and ∨ (OR), e.g. (C ∨ D) ∧ ¬B. From the keyboard you can type !, & and | instead: (C | D) & !B. Transitions are evaluated whenever at least one input spikes.
  4. Click a state to mark it as active at start. Several states may be active at the same time.
  5. Check the generated network in Network and run it in Simulate.

How to use it
  1. Hover over a neuron to highlight its synapses, or over a synapse to highlight only that synapse.
  2. Click a neuron to see its synapses and when it spikes after an input spike (path in blue), or a synapse to see its type, weight and delay. Click the background to close it.
  3. Double-click a name or a column heading to rename it.
  4. Drag or scroll to move, Ctrl + scroll to zoom.
  5. Choose the implementation below, and show or hide neuron types and synapses.

Advanced parameters

Duration of one step t: every synaptic delay is one step and input spikes are multiples of it. The neuron time constants of Table 1 (for 1 ms) are scaled with it, so the network behaves the same with any step. The Ideal simulator accepts any value. SpiNNaker accepts any whole number of microseconds; below 1 ms it runs slower than real time.

Empty: automatic, the simulation ends 30 time steps after the last input spike. Input spikes at or after the end are left out.

SpiNNaker runs this many times slower than real time (one time step lasts step × factor of wall-clock time). Empty: automatic, the smallest factor that SpiNNaker supports (1 for steps of 1 ms or more, ⌈1 ms / step⌉ below). A larger factor gives the board more time for each step.
